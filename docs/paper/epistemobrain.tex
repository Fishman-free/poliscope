\begin{abstract}

Scientific literature review is among the highest-value uses of large
language model (LLM) agents, and also among the riskiest. A fluent,
well-cited summary can hide a causal claim built on cross-sectional
correlation, six papers that draw on a single dataset, or a dissenting view
that the summarizer quietly averaged away. We present \textbf{EpistemoBrain},
an agentic framework in which \textbf{seven AI scientists with distinct
specializations} review a contested empirical question under a formal
council protocol (independent precommitment, professional acquisition,
evidence exchange, cross-examination, blindspot bounty, joint modeling, and
final rejudgment), and produce an \emph{auditable controversy evidence map}:
conclusions sit next to their limitations, every claim traces to its source,
and no dissent is deleted.

EpistemoBrain treats memory as the public cognitive layer of a scientific
community rather than a private per-agent cache. Three layers, private
process memory, collective executive memory, and a formal evidence graph,
are organized as a \emph{dual-graph} architecture: the process graph records
how the scientists worked, while the evidence graph records what the
community now knows. Every formal change passes through an append-only
Scientific Event Ledger, a six-stage Evidence Gate, and a single Graph
Projector whose exclusive write privilege is enforced at the database level.
The protocol never uses majority voting to settle scientific questions.
Outcomes take the form of conditional consensus, and genuine disagreement
persists as named Dissent Certificates.

We evaluate EpistemoBrain on the \textbf{ForesightBlindspot} benchmark, a
time-sliced evaluation protocol for blindspot discovery in computational
social science. The study comprises a controlled scripted-case comparison of
five baselines (single-agent deep research, fixed multi-agent debate, a
council with linear context, a council with executive memory but no evidence
engine, and the full system) and a six-variant ablation ladder that removes
one capability at a time. Blindspot coverage follows seat coverage: a lone
researcher misses six of seven specialist blindspots. Removing the
adversarial falsifier or the evidence auditor each costs a gold blindspot,
and removing lineage tracking inflates the system's own evidence-independence
report. A real-model study further shows that keyword-based blindspot recall
fails on free-form text and that a semantic judge repairs the measurement.
Across repeated runs we find a stable precision--recall trade-off rather
than sampling noise: the full system's governance attains perfect blindspot
precision at the price of the lowest recall (two of seven gold concepts),
every simplified variant, including the lone researcher, finds nearly all
gold concepts at lower precision, and removing precommitment restores recall
1.0 in every run. End-to-end verification on real research tasks is reported
alongside.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

LLM agents have made deep research practical: a user asks a question, and an
agent retrieves, reads, and synthesizes the literature into a coherent answer
\citep{yao2023react,lewis2020retrieval}. For contested scientific questions,
the standard pipeline carries three failure modes that a fluency-optimized
summary cannot repair. A single agent that reads a paper tends to see only
its own interpretation; the measurement scientist's concern about
self-report bias is invisible to a pipeline that has no measurement
scientist. Agreement is not evidence either. If seven agents read the same
erroneous abstract, the same retracted paper, or the same misattributed
citation, the system can package a \emph{shared error} as a consensus that
looks verified simply because it was repeated
\citep{ioannidis2005why,osc2015estimating}. Finally, a summarizer's closing
``everyone basically agrees'' step erases exactly the information a
contested review must preserve: what the dissenting scientist opposed, and
which evidence would change their mind. These are not prompt-tuning problems.
They are structural questions about how a scientific review should be
organized.

Computational social science makes the stakes concrete. The reproducibility
crisis has shown that a large share of published findings do not replicate
\citep{osc2015estimating}, that publication bias inflates apparent effect
sizes \citep{fanelli2010positive}, and that researcher degrees of freedom
multiply false positives \citep{gelman2013garden,nosek2018preregistration}.
In the domain this work targets, digital behavior, social media, and mental
health, cross-sectional designs are routinely reported as causal, self-report
measures dominate, and a small number of datasets support a large fraction of
the literature \citep{orben2019association,valkenburg2022social}. An evidence
reviewer for this domain needs more than an accurate summary. It has to
\emph{audit} claims against their sources, \emph{count independent evidence}
rather than papers, and \emph{keep the dissent} visible.

We propose \textbf{EpistemoBrain}, the organizing brain of \textbf{Poliscope}.
It is a deep research agent that assembles \textbf{seven AI scientists}:
theory builder, causal scientist, measurement and construct scientist,
statistics and replication scientist, boundary and context scientist,
adversarial falsifier, and evidence and provenance auditor. The scientists
interact through a seven-round council protocol. The design starts from the
opposite direction of ``seven chatbots plus a summarizer'': we first decide
what organizational structure and memory mechanisms a reliable controversy
review requires, and then place the roles inside that structure. Four pillars
carry the architecture. First, all seven seats participate in every task;
cost is controlled through speaking budgets, semantic deduplication, and
shared retrieval caches rather than by dropping seats. Second, dissent is a
first-class object. Majority voting is forbidden, outcomes are conditional
consensus, and dissents become named certificates. Third, process and
evidence live in separate graphs, coupled by an append-only event ledger and
a single database-enforced writer. Fourth, memory is a layered public
cognitive layer (private per-seat memory, collective executive memory, and
the formal evidence graph), with MemoBrain's native operations redefined in
scientific semantics as Quarantine, Dialectical Fold, and Perspective Recall.

We evaluate EpistemoBrain on \textbf{ForesightBlindspot}, a time-sliced
evaluation protocol for blindspot discovery in computational social science.
The evidence comes from a controlled comparison of five baselines and a
six-variant ablation ladder on a scripted case with no model calls, no
network, and no database, together with end-to-end verification on real
research tasks. Blindspot coverage follows seat coverage: after we corrected
the single-agent baseline's seat-selection semantics (Section~4.2), it finds
one of seven gold blindspots, while every seven-seat variant finds all seven.
The ablations locate the seats that carry coverage (removing the adversarial
falsifier or the evidence auditor each costs a gold blindspot) and expose a
self-report trap: removing lineage inflates the system's own
evidence-independence score. Under scripted gateways, the remaining
mechanisms (memory, evidence gate) are not observable on the blindspot
metric, and we identify this as a boundary of the measurement rather than a
claim about the mechanisms.

Our contributions are threefold:
(1) We introduce a \emph{council protocol} for scientific controversy review.
Seven specialized scientist agents interact through structured scientific
actions (propose, support, challenge, qualify, fork, request, revise,
dissent) under an explicit prohibition on majority voting, producing
conditional consensus with minority positions preserved.
(2) We design \emph{dual-graph evidence governance}: a process graph and an
evidence graph coupled by an append-only, idempotent, replayable Scientific
Event Ledger, a six-stage Evidence Gate, and a single Graph Projector whose
write privileges are enforced at the database privilege level. A layered
memory model redefines executive-memory operations in scientific semantics.
(3) We release \textbf{Poliscope}, an open-source implementation, together
with the \textbf{ForesightBlindspot} evaluation protocol, a five-baseline
comparison, and end-to-end verification, establishing a measurement basis
for blindspot discovery in computational social science.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Multi-Agent Debate and Collaboration}

Multi-agent debate improves reasoning by having agents critique one
another's answers \citep{du2023improving,liang2023encouraging,chen2023agentverse,
qian2024chatdev}, and role-specialized panels have been proposed for domain
reasoning. These systems typically close with a summarization step that
aggregates positions, and none addresses the failure mode we consider
central: the summarizer can flatten a genuine dissent, and the agents can
converge on a shared evidence error that looks like independent verification.
EpistemoBrain differs on both axes. It forbids majority voting as a truth
mechanism, and it subjects evidence, rather than arguments, to a six-stage
gate before anything enters the formal knowledge graph.

\subsection{Memory for Long-Horizon Agents}

Long-horizon reasoning strains bounded contexts, which motivates explicit
memory architectures \citep{packer2023memgpt,sumers2023coala}. MemoBrain
introduces executive memory for tool-augmented agents: a dependency-aware
graph of reasoning steps with Flush, Fold, and Recall operations that prune
invalid steps, fold completed sub-trajectories, and preserve a compact,
high-salience reasoning backbone under a fixed context budget
\citep{qian2026memobrain}. EpistemoBrain adopts MemoBrain as its
executive-memory foundation but changes the unit of analysis. MemoBrain
manages \emph{one} agent's trajectory; EpistemoBrain manages a
\emph{community}'s shared cognitive state. Three redefinitions follow. Flush
becomes Quarantine: a refuted claim changes status instead of disappearing.
Fold becomes Dialectical Fold: a dispute compresses only after both strongest
positions, the hinge variables, and the falsification conditions are
preserved. Recall becomes Perspective Recall: each seat recalls a
role-specific projection of the shared evidence graph. Process memory and
formal evidence are additionally separated into two graphs, because a
scientist's momentary guess must never quietly upgrade into a formal
conclusion.

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation and Factual Verification}

RAG grounds generation in retrieved documents \citep{lewis2020retrieval}, and
verification pipelines check claims against evidence
\citep{thorne2018fever,wadden2020scifact,min2023factscore,dhuliawala2023chain}.
These systems verify \emph{textual entailment}. Auditing a \emph{scientific
claim} requires checks that standard entailment systems do not perform:
evidence levels (full text with exact quotes versus abstract-only metadata),
citation entailment (an introductory hypothesis must not be reported as a
tested result), causal-upgrade policy (cross-sectional correlation must not
become a causal claim), evidence independence (papers sharing a dataset count
as one independent evidence cluster), and source provenance (retraction
status, preprint lineage). We reuse the production rules for these audits in
our scoring functions rather than re-deriving parallel approximations.

\subsection{LLM Agents for Science}

LLM agents are increasingly applied to scientific tasks, from hypothesis
generation to literature analysis. A recurring concern is that agentic
systems can produce authoritative-sounding claims without grounding
\citep{ioannidis2005why,osc2015estimating}. Our response is to make grounding
a governance property rather than a prompt property. We are not aware of a
system that combines a seven-scientist council protocol, dual-graph
governance, an append-only event ledger with database-enforced write
isolation, and a time-sliced blindspot evaluation benchmark in one
open-source implementation. To our knowledge, Poliscope is the first.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Overview and Formalization}

Figure~\ref{fig:overview} gives the architecture. A researcher submits a
Research Contract: the question, scope, budget, and an optional knowledge
base and set of PDFs. The system proposes candidate claims, and the
researcher confirms which ones to investigate. A worker then runs the
seven-seat council through its seven deliberative rounds (eight phases when
the fixed human checkpoint is counted), appending every formal change as an
event to the Scientific Event Ledger. The Graph Projector, the ledger's only
consumer and the evidence graph's only writer, applies the six-stage Evidence
Gate to each candidate and materializes the admitted nodes and edges. Gaps in
the evidence graph generate blindspot bounties, which are scored, ranked, and
broadcast back into the council (epistemic routing). The researcher finishes
with a Research Brief, an interactive controversy map, an audit trail, and a
synthesized final paper.

Following the formalization style of executive-memory models, we make the
data flow explicit. A task is a contract $X=(q, s, b, K)$ over a question
$q$, scope $s$, budget $b$, and optional materials $K$. At step $t$ a seat
issues a structured action $a_t$; every formal change is encoded as an event
\begin{equation}
e_t = (t,\ \mathrm{actor},\ \mathrm{type},\ \mathrm{payload},\ \mathrm{refs}),
\end{equation}
and the ledger grows only by admission:
\begin{equation}
L_{t+1} =
\begin{cases}
L_t \oplus e_t, & \mathrm{Gate}(e_t) = \mathrm{admit},\\
L_t, & \mathrm{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\oplus$ is idempotent append keyed by a deterministic idempotency key.
The evidence graph is a pure projection of the admitted ledger,
\begin{equation}
G = \mathrm{Project}(L),
\end{equation}
so replaying the ledger on an empty database reconstructs $G$ exactly, and no
other code path can write it. Each seat's working context is a role-specific
sum of process recall and an evidence projection,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_i =\;& \mathrm{ProcessRecall}_i(L)\\
&+ \mathrm{EvidenceProjection}_i(G, \mathrm{role}_i).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
which is why two seats never read the same undifferentiated summary. A gap
$u$ exposed by $G$ becomes a bounty scored on five dimensions,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bounty}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{b}(u) =\;& w_1\,\mathrm{impact}(u)
+ w_2\,\mathrm{uncertainty}(u)\\
&+ w_3\,\mathrm{investigability}(u)
+ w_4\,\mathrm{novelty}(u)\\
&- w_5\,\widehat{\mathrm{cost}}(u).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and the final round either produces a conditional consensus
\begin{equation}
C^{*} = \mathrm{JointModel}(\{C_i\}_{i=1}^{7}, G),
\label{eq:consensus}
\end{equation}
or refuses when the strongest opposing position or a falsification condition
is missing. Equations~(2)--(3) encode the write isolation of
Section~3.4, Eq.~\eqref{eq:bounty} drives the routing of Section~3.5, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:consensus} is the no-voting rule of Section~3.2 made formal.

\subsection{The Seven-Scientist Council}

Each research task runs all seven seats. Table~\ref{tab:seats} lists the
seats, the question each owns, and its primary responsibilities. Every seat
has an independent role specification, independent private state and private
memory, and a different evidence-projection weighting. Seats gather evidence
without seeing one another and commit positions first; evidence is exchanged
only afterward, so cross-examination examines genuinely independent
positions rather than restating a shared draft.

Scientists submit only structured actions: \texttt{PROPOSE},
\texttt{SUPPORT}, \texttt{CHALLENGE}, \texttt{QUALIFY}, \texttt{FORK},
\texttt{REQUEST}, \texttt{REVISE}, \texttt{DISSENT}; when challenged they may
\texttt{DEFEND}, \texttt{REVISE}, \texttt{NARROW}, \texttt{WITHDRAW}, or
\texttt{DISSENT}. Every action carries an actor, a target, a statement,
evidence references, a confidence, a falsification condition, and a novelty
mark. The ledger rejects factual assertions without evidence, target-less
comments, duplicated positions, and agreement that adds no information.

The protocol runs seven rounds.
(1) \emph{Independent Precommitment}: seats commit initial judgments and
confidence levels without seeing one another (sealed first, read later).
(2) \emph{Professional Acquisition}: seats issue evidence requests from
their own perspectives; a query planner merges them and executes against
shared sources (OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, Unpaywall, and
user-provided materials).
(3) \emph{Evidence Exchange}: structured evidence is exchanged, never
private reasoning histories.
(4) \emph{Cross-Examination}: targeted challenges on causality, measurement,
statistics, boundaries, sources, and hidden assumptions.
(5) \emph{Blindspot Bounty}: gaps exposed by the evidence graph are scored on
impact, uncertainty, investigability, novelty, and cost, then ranked and
claimed.
(6) \emph{Joint Modeling}: a conditional consensus is drafted together with
the shared understanding, the strongest supporting and opposing positions,
hinge variables, scope boundaries, unresolved conflicts, and falsification
conditions.
(7) \emph{Final Rejudgment}: seats judge independently again, recording
belief updates and named dissents.

\emph{No majority vote decides scientific truth.} The joint-modeling round
refuses to produce a consensus when the strongest opposing position or a
falsification condition is missing. A seat that cannot accept the consensus
receives a Dissent Certificate, a first-class node in the evidence graph
that records what it opposes, why, and what evidence would retract the
dissent.

\subsection{Layered Memory and Dialectical Operations}

Memory is organized in three layers. \emph{Private memory} holds each
scientist's tool calls, failed routes, private hypotheses, and plans.
\emph{Collective executive memory} records the community's structured
cognitive events: who proposed what, who attacked whom, which controversies
remain open, and which blindspots nobody has claimed. \emph{The evidence
graph} holds the audited formal knowledge: atomic claims, study findings,
sources, constructs, contexts, blindspots, debate capsules, and dissent
certificates.

MemoBrain's native operations are redefined for the scientific setting.
\emph{Quarantine} (from Flush) keeps a refuted claim instead of deleting it:
the claim moves through \texttt{PROPOSED $\rightarrow$ SUPPORTED $\rightarrow$
CONTESTED $\rightarrow$ QUARANTINED $\rightarrow$
RESURRECTED/REJECTED}, and its record keeps who refuted it, what evidence is
missing, and which conditions would resurrect it. \emph{Dialectical Fold}
(from Fold) compresses a dispute into a Debate Capsule only after preserving
the shared understanding, the strongest supporting evidence, the strongest
opposing evidence, hinge variables, scope boundaries, unresolved conflicts,
and falsification conditions; the original nodes remain, and the capsule
links back to them. \emph{Perspective Recall} (from Recall) builds seat
$i$'s context as in Eq.~(4): a role-specific slice of the shared evidence
graph added to that seat's process recall, so the seven seats never read one
undifferentiated summary. Three dynamics complete the set. \emph{Fork} opens
parallel research paths when evidence cannot distinguish competing
explanations. \emph{Resurrect} re-activates a quarantined hypothesis when new
evidence meets its resurrection conditions. \emph{Merge} records conditional
merge candidates for the researcher to adjudicate.

\subsection{Dual-Graph Governance}

Process and conclusion are stored apart. The \emph{Process Graph} answers
``how did the scientists work'': tasks, tool calls, failed routes,
challenges, and decisions, managed by MemoBrain and freely foldable. The
\emph{Evidence Graph} answers ``what do we now know'': ten node types
(ResearchQuestion, Claim, Source, StudyFinding, Construct, Context,
Blindspot, DebateCapsule, DiscriminatingStudy, DissentCertificate) and twelve
edge types (SUPPORTS, REFUTES, QUALIFIES, CONTRADICTS, CONFOUNDS, MEDIATES,
MODERATES, OPERATIONALIZES, DERIVED\_FROM, APPLIES\_IN, EXPOSES, TESTS).

The two graphs are coupled by the \emph{Scientific Event Ledger}. Every
formal change is first appended as an idempotent, replayable, auditable
event, and the \emph{Graph Projector}, the ledger's only consumer and the
evidence graph's only writer, applies the Evidence Gate and materializes the
admitted nodes and edges (Eqs.~(2)--(3)). Write isolation is enforced at the
database privilege level, not by coding discipline. The application role
holds no insert, update, or truncate privilege on the evidence-graph tables,
and the projector role holds no privilege on the ledger, so a bug on either
side cannot forge the other's data. Refuted, quarantined, and folded nodes
are never physically deleted; they change status and remain traceable.

\subsection{The Evidence Gate}

Every candidate event passes six stages: \emph{schema validation},
\emph{semantic deduplication}, \emph{source verification}, \emph{citation
entailment}, \emph{method quality}, and \emph{graph consistency}. Evidence is
graded A through D. Level A requires full text with exact quotes and precise
location; Level B is abstract plus metadata; Level C covers second-hand
descriptions in reviews; Level D covers web or news leads. Levels B, C, and D
can never alone support a high-confidence causal conclusion. Three audits
apply to formal findings: source authenticity (DOI, title, authors, version,
retraction, PDF identity), citation entailment (whether the quote supports
the claim, whether correlation is reported as causation, whether qualifiers
were dropped), and method quality (directness, design strength, measurement
validity, statistical precision, replicability, external validity). A
\emph{CausalUpgradePolicy} intercepts the combination of a cross-sectional
design with a causal claim at stage six and quarantines the event with an
audit record. The pipeline also tracks evidence independence. Lineage
detection classifies same-dataset, overlapping-sample, preprint, extension,
reanalysis, and meta-analysis-inclusion dependencies, and the interface
reports both the paper count and the independent-evidence-cluster count: six
papers sharing one dataset count as one independent evidence cluster.

Four mechanisms defend against the subtler failure of \emph{shared} evidence
errors. \emph{Blind evidence review} strips prestige signals (author,
journal, citation counts) from first substantive judgments. \emph{Independent
dual extraction} extracts high-impact findings twice through separate paths
and escalates discrepancies. \emph{Source-diversity constraints} generate a
Blindspot node automatically when all supporting evidence for a claim shares
a dataset, team, country, or design. \emph{Adversarial retrieval} runs six
classes of reverse queries (refutation, null results, alternative theories,
measurement criticism, replication failure, boundary inversion) against the
evidence sources for each confirmed claim.

\subsection{Epistemic Routing and Blindspot Bounty}

The council is not a scripted play-through of seven rounds. Gaps exposed by
the evidence graph generate blindspot bounties (Eq.~\eqref{eq:bounty}),
which are scored, ranked, and broadcast for seats to claim. The evidence
state, rather than a moderator reading from a script, drives the next
investigation direction. A blindspot is consequently a first-class object in
the product (a radar view ranked by impact and investigability), not a
limitations paragraph. When a bounty round closes, the system may also
propose a \emph{DiscriminatingStudy}: a design with competing predictions
that would resolve an open conflict, together with its expected information
gain.

\subsection{Human Directional Checkpoint}

After the blindspot bounty and before joint modeling, the task pauses at one
fixed checkpoint (\texttt{AWAITING\_COUNCIL\_INPUT}). The researcher may
review the seven seats' precommitment positions and submit a short
directional note (for example, ``prioritize cross-cultural boundary
conditions''), or explicitly leave it blank and continue; both paths are
equally valid. The note enters joint modeling only under the explicit label
``researcher directional note, not a scientific judgment''. It never changes
an Evidence Gate decision, never serves as evidence for or against a claim,
and never enters a Debate Capsule or Dissent Certificate. Identical evidence
input must produce identical claim admission, dissents, and gate decisions
with or without a note, a property asserted by tests. The checkpoint is
directional control, not an eighth vote.

\subsection{Engineering Realization}

Poliscope is a modular monolith with an asynchronous worker and PostgreSQL as
the source of truth; no workflow state lives only in memory. The worker
claims tasks with \texttt{SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED}. A council run
executes each phase in one transaction as the application role and commits;
the projector then runs in a second transaction as the projector role. Two
identities, two transactions, one ledger. All model calls go through a Model
Gateway (OpenAI-compatible endpoints, streaming with a non-streaming
fallback, per-task model configuration, cost and latency accounting), and
all external tools go through a Tool Gateway (OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic
Scholar, Unpaywall, user knowledge bases). A single-seat failure degrades the
round: the seat is marked absent with a reason on the ledger instead of
aborting the task. Structured-output repair failures are quarantined and
never write the formal graph. Live views are served as two SSE streams, the
formal ledger stream and a best-effort process stream for token-level
progress, so the audit trail is never polluted by reasoning noise. The
implementation is open-source (MIT) at
\url{https://github.com/Fishman-free/poliscope}, archived at
\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22541350}.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{The ForesightBlindspot Benchmark and Baselines}

We design \textbf{ForesightBlindspot}, a time-sliced evaluation protocol for
blindspot discovery in computational social science (domain: digital
behavior, social media, and mental health). Each case is a contested question
with a cutoff date: the system may read only the pre-cutoff corpus. Gold
blindspots are gaps that later high-quality research confirmed, that had
reasonable premonitory evidence before the cutoff, and that do not depend on
entirely unforeseeable platforms, events, or methods. Leakage controls are
built into the protocol: closed pre-cutoff corpora, no future citations,
anonymizable variables, and an explicit disclosure that base-model parameters
may contain future knowledge, a risk that cannot be fully eliminated. The
metric family covers blindspots (recall, precision, high-impact recall,
specificity, actionability, foresight distance), evidence (citation
existence, citation entailment, evidence independence, causal-overclaim rate,
full-text coverage), memory (compression ratio, scientific-backbone
retention, contradiction retention, long-horizon drift, recovery quality),
collaboration (role diversity, debate utility, belief-update quality, dissent
preservation, false-consensus rate), and cost (cost per valid blindspot,
tokens per admitted finding).

The five baselines are configurations of the real council rather than
separate implementations, so each adds exactly one capability over the
previous: (1) \emph{Single-Agent Deep Research}, one researcher (the theory
builder) with no protocol, no memory, and no gate; (2) \emph{Fixed
Multi-Agent Debate}, seven seats with an undifferentiated deliberator, no
per-seat memory, and no gate; (3) \emph{Council + Linear Context}, seven
seats sharing one undifferentiated transcript; (4) \emph{Council + MemoBrain
without Evidence Engine}, seven seats with per-seat memory but the gate
removed; and (5) \emph{Full Poliscope}, the complete system.

\subsection{Baseline Construction Correctness}

Before reporting numbers, we validated that the baselines are what they claim
to be, and the first evaluation run exposed two construction defects. First,
the single-agent baseline selected its seat as the alphabetically first seat
of an ordered enumeration, which happened to be the adversarial falsifier.
The ``lone researcher'' was in fact the falsifier, and in our scripted
material the falsifier holds the strongest blindspot answers, so the single
agent scored as high as the full council (F1 0.8 on all five variants).
Second, the final-rejudgment handler iterated all seven seats
unconditionally, minting placeholder judgments for seats that never
participated. We corrected both: the single-agent baseline explicitly uses
the theory builder, and the rejudgment handler respects the
participating-seat set with a compatible fallback that leaves the seven-seat
production path byte-for-byte unchanged. A regression guard now asserts that
single-seat blindspot coverage is strictly below multi-seat coverage, so a
future regression that collapses the baseline ladder fails immediately. We
report this correction because the construction of the measurement instrument
is part of the measurement.

\subsection{Main Results on the Scripted Case}

Table~\ref{tab:results} reports the five baselines on a scripted demo case
(screen time and adolescent depressive symptoms) in which every gateway is
deterministic: no model calls, no network, no database. Each seat nominates
one specialist blindspot in the bounty round (the falsifier nominates two,
one deliberately duplicating the boundary scientist's nomination to model
overlapping multi-seat nominations). Gold blindspots are one keyword per
specialist seat.

Three findings stand out. First, blindspot coverage is driven by \emph{seat
coverage}. The lone researcher finds one of seven specialist blindspots
(F1 0.222), while every seven-seat variant finds all seven (F1 0.875). The
council's contribution is real and large, and it is attributable to the set
of seats rather than to the presence of any single one. Second, what matters
is the \emph{set of participating seats}, not a particular seat: after the
construction fix, the single agent's score is determined by what its own seat
contributes, not by whether the falsifier happens to be present. Third, the
four seven-seat variants score identically on the blindspot metric under
scripted gateways, and this is a structural consequence of the harness. Every
variant collects all seven seats' outputs, the scripted gateway ignores
prompts and memory, and bounty events are admitted unconditionally by the
gate. The contributions of the protocol, memory, and evidence gate to
blindspot \emph{integration} are therefore not observable through this metric
on scripted material. They surface in the auxiliary scores (dissent
preservation, citation entailment, evidence independence) and require
real-model runs to observe on blindspots. We treat this as an identified
boundary of the measurement, not as a claim about the mechanisms.

\subsection{Ablation Study}

Beyond the five-baseline ladder, we ablate the full system one capability at
a time (design spec 11.4): six \emph{full minus X} variants, each removing
exactly one mechanism while everything else, including the evidence gate,
stays intact. The removals are (1)~independent precommitment (the phase does
not run); (2)~the adversarial falsifier seat; (3)~the evidence auditor seat;
(4)~the dialectical fold (JOINT MODELING skips the DebateCapsule and records
an unfilled slot instead of silently dropping the opposition); (5)~evidence
lineage (acquired sources arrive without dataset metadata, so evidence
independence cannot see that two papers share one dataset); (6)~MemoBrain
(per-seat private memory is replaced by one undifferentiated transcript).
Each ablation is a configuration of the same orchestrator the full system
runs. The switch is one flag or one seat subset, not a reimplementation, so
the contrast with Full Poliscope isolates that capability's contribution.

Table~\ref{tab:ablations} shows three mechanism-level effects. Removing the
adversarial falsifier drops recall from 1.000 to 0.857: the publication-bias
blindspot was its nomination, and no other seat covers it, so a council
without a dedicated falsifier misses exactly the gap that role exists to
find. Precision rises at the same time (0.778 to 0.857), because the
falsifier's second nomination deliberately duplicates the boundary
scientist's, and without the falsifier no statement is double-paid. Removing
the evidence auditor also costs recall (1.000 to 0.857, F1 0.800): the
provenance blindspot belonged to that seat. Removing lineage raises evidence
independence from 0.667 to 1.000. Without dataset metadata, two papers that
share a cohort are counted as independent evidence, so the number the system
reports about its own evidence quality is \emph{inflated by the absence of
the very mechanism that would correct it}. The dialectical-fold ablation
cannot move the blindspot metric, but it is visible in the honesty signal:
the DebateCapsule disappears and the run records one additional unfilled slot
rather than pretending the debate had no opposition. Dissent preservation is
a property of the protocol, and its absence is recorded, not hidden.

Two ablations show no effect on this scripted material, and we say so
plainly rather than manufacturing a difference. Removing precommitment
removes the seven PRECOMMITMENT\_SEALED events but leaves every score
unchanged; removing MemoBrain changes nothing either. This is the same
structural boundary identified for the baseline ladder: a scripted gateway
answers identically regardless of prompt, memory, or phase history, so
protocol-level integration effects (what precommitment changes about later
reasoning, what per-seat memory changes about what a seat recalls) are not
observable without real models. The three ablations that do show effects
(falsifier, auditor, lineage) do so because they change a \emph{participant}
with scripted content or the data a scoring function sees, not because the
mechanism's behavioral consequences are being measured. We therefore read the
three effects as validated mechanism checks and the two null results as an
identified measurement boundary, not as evidence that the two capabilities
contribute nothing.

\subsection{Real-Model Evaluation and the Semantic Judge}

The scripted case is deterministic, and its blindspot metric rests on a
\emph{keyword} proxy: ``reverse\_causation'' matches only statements that
literally contain those words (underscore-separated and case-folded). The
scripted gateway writes its statements to contain the keyword, so the proxy
works there. It does not survive contact with a real model. Free-form output
such as ``the direction of effect could run the other way'' names the
reverse-causation gap without containing the token, so the keyword score
reports recall 0.0 even when the gap was found. To measure blindspot quality
under a real model we added a \emph{semantic judge}: for each gold concept,
the same model gateway (no second vendor SDK) is asked which numbered
candidate statements semantically address that concept, and recall and
precision are derived with the identical definitions the keyword score uses.
A judge that is unreachable, or whose structured answer is quarantined,
returns \emph{unmeasured} rather than silently 0.0.

We then ran five key variants (the full system, the single-agent baseline,
and the three mechanism ablations whose scripted effects were largest)
through the real gateway (DeepSeek-V4-Flash on the official DeepSeek API),
three runs each, and scored blindspots with the semantic judge. An initial
eleven-variant single-run sweep was abandoned after the first endpoint
rate-limited for two days and polluted twelve runs (unfilled slots jumped to
46, and the judge was unreachable for every semantic call); those runs were
discarded rather than reported. Table~\ref{tab:real} reports the three-run
means (F1 is recomputed from mean recall and precision, not averaged over
per-run F1s).

Three findings follow. \emph{First}, the semantic judge repairs the
measurement: the keyword proxy reports recall 0.0 for the full system on
free-form text, while the semantic judge produces a readable, non-degenerate
distribution. \emph{Second}, repeated runs turn the earlier single-run
reading (``sampling variance dominates'') into a stable, reproducible
\emph{precision--recall trade-off}. Full Poliscope achieves perfect precision
($1.000$) at the cost of the lowest recall ($0.286$, exactly two of seven
gold concepts in all three runs). Every simplified variant, including the
lone researcher, finds nearly all gold concepts (recall $0.809$--$1.000$) at
markedly lower precision ($0.406$--$0.586$). Removing precommitment
reproduces recall $1.000$ in every run, so the earlier single-run
observation was a real, repeated effect rather than a sampling artifact.
Unfilled slots explain the gap: the full system leaves 12.3 slots unfilled on
average (the model produced no structured nomination), against 1.0--1.7 for
the simplified variants. Protocol burden on a flash-tier model is the binding
constraint: governance buys precision at the cost of recall, and under a weak
model the recall cost dominates F1. \emph{Third}, the deterministic
data-level scores (citation entailment $0.5$, evidence independence $0.667$,
dissent preservation $1.0$) are identical across all fifteen runs, as
expected for scoring that does not depend on model output.

\subsection{Analysis: What the Trade-off Says About Deployment}

The real-model results are not a negative result, and reading them as one
would miss the paper's central design claim. The governance mechanisms
EpistemoBrain adds operate at the \emph{integration} level: they shape what a
seat reasons and recalls across rounds, and their benefit needs a model
strong enough to sustain the full protocol without the seat absence observed
here (12.3 unfilled slots against 1.0--1.7). What the repeated runs
establish is a deployment map. When evidence quality is the priority (a
contested review where an admitted causal claim can be wrong in a costly
way), the full protocol is the only configuration with perfect blindspot
precision and intact honesty signals: dissent preservation $1.0$ across all
fifteen runs, citation entailment and evidence independence computed from
data rather than asserted. When coverage is the priority and the available
model is a flash-tier one, a lighter protocol (or precommitment removed)
recovers recall $0.809$--$1.000$ at a known, quantified precision cost. The
scripted case isolates each mechanism's structural contribution; the
real-model runs locate the model-strength boundary at which that isolation
stops. Both pieces are needed to choose a configuration honestly, and the
system reports whichever side of the trade-off it is running on instead of
averaging the two into a single score.

\subsection{System Verification}

Beyond the controlled comparison, Poliscope is verified end to end. The test
suite comprises 752 unit tests and 364 integration tests over a real
PostgreSQL instance with three database identities, including privilege tests
that assert the application role cannot write the evidence graph and the
projector role cannot rewrite the ledger: dual-graph isolation is a database
fact, not a code convention. Real research tasks run through the full
seven-seat council on real OpenAI-compatible endpoints, producing conditional
consensus, Debate Capsules, Dissent Certificates, and a synthesized final
paper whose references are bound to DOIs. Tasks that exhaust budget or lose
seats finish as ``completed with gaps'', with every absent seat and unfilled
slot named in the report rather than fabricated into a success. The event
ledger is idempotent and replay-safe (re-running a task is a no-op), and the
streaming live view survives reconnection via Last-Event-ID.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented EpistemoBrain, the organizing brain of the open-source Poliscope
system: a seven-scientist council with a formal protocol, layered memory,
dual-graph evidence governance, an append-only event ledger with
database-enforced write isolation, an evidence gate with three-layer audit,
and epistemic routing through blindspot bounties. The design thesis is that
reliable blindspot discovery is an organizational and governance problem
rather than a prompt problem. Dissent has to survive the pipeline, evidence
has to be audited before it becomes a conclusion, and a process note has to
stay distinguishable from evidence. The ForesightBlindspot protocol, the
five-baseline comparison, and the six-variant ablation ladder establish a
measurement basis, with one open caveat: scripted gateways make the
protocol's integration-level contributions unobservable on the blindspot
metric, which is exactly why two of the six ablations are null on this
material. The real-model study shows that a keyword proxy for blindspot
recall fails on free-form text and that a semantic judge repairs the
measurement; repeated runs then expose a stable deployment boundary rather
than noise. Under a flash-tier model, full governance buys perfect blindspot
precision at the cost of recall (every simplified variant, including a lone
researcher, finds more gold concepts), and seat absence (12.3 unfilled slots
against 1.0--1.7) is the binding constraint. Observing the integration-level
benefits therefore requires either a stronger model or a deliberately lighter
protocol, and the system is designed to report which regime it is in.

\section{Appendix: Implementation Notes}

\textbf{Deterministic evaluation harness.} The ForesightBlindspot harness
(\texttt{packages/evaluation/}) runs the five baselines and the six
ablation variants as configurations of the real orchestrator against a
database-less event sink, so a run is deterministic and fast enough for
unit tests; scoring functions reuse the production audit rules (causal
upgrade policy, citation entailment verifier, lineage detection and
evidence clustering) instead of re-deriving them, so a variant's score
reflects the same standard the gated system is held to. Each ablation is
one protocol switch on that same orchestrator -- a phase subset, a seat
subset, a fold flag, or a lineage-free acquirer -- never a reimplementation
(Section~4.4). Gold blindspot matching is implemented as a keyword
proxy, with a double-annotation agreement protocol (Kappa/Alpha
statistics, adjudication) in place.

\textbf{Permissions as design.} PostgreSQL grants implement the write
isolation: the application role can append to the ledger and read the
evidence graph but cannot insert, update, or truncate graph tables; the
projector role can write the graph and advance event status but cannot
rewrite event payloads; neither role can truncate anything. A documented
exception grants the application role DELETE for the session-lifecycle
operation of destroying a whole task at the researcher's explicit request
-- the one path by which evidence rows may be physically removed.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} The repository pins migrations (Alembic),
seeds, and Docker Compose services (postgres, migrate, api, worker, web,
caddy); the demonstration task seed runs the real orchestrator with
scripted model and tool stand-ins, and the evaluation harness runs with
scripted gateways, so all numbers in this paper are reproducible without
external credentials.
